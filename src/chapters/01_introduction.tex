\chapter{Introduction}\label{chap:introduction}

Quantum computing uses strange but powerful phenomena from \gls{quantum-mechanics}, like \gls{superposition} and \gls{entanglement}, to perform certain computations much faster than \gls{classical-computer}s ever could \parencite{Berberich2023QuantumCT, Liu2023IntroductionTQ}. While this opens the door to major breakthroughs, it also threatens one of the core pillars of modern digital life: \gls{cryptography}.

Some quantum algorithms hit particularly hard. \gls{shors-algorithm} \parencite{shor1997polynomial} breaks the math behind today's most widely used \gls{public-key-cryptography} systems \gls{rsa}, \gls{ecc}, and \gls{dh}. all in \gls{polynomial-time}. \gls{grovers-algorithm} doesn't break \gls{symmetric-cipher}s outright, but it still weakens them by giving a quadratic speedup in \gls{brute-force-attack}s, effectively halving their security.

The threat isn't theoretical anymore. The "Store Now, Decrypt Later" (\gls{sndl}) scenario is real: attackers can harvest encrypted data now and wait for quantum machines to catch up \parencite{nist_pqc_timeline_2024, Geremew2024PreparingCI, Regenscheid2024TransitionTP}. That's why we need to move towards \gls{post-quantum-cryptography} (\gls{pqc}) algorithms designed to hold up against both classical and quantum attacks. But switching over isn't as easy as it sounds. It means rethinking systems, protocols, standards, basically, rebuilding mostly from the ground up \parencite{nist_pqc_timeline_2024, Geremew2024PreparingCI, Regenscheid2024TransitionTP}.

This memoire looks at how quantum computing changes the game for \gls{cryptography}. It covers the weaknesses exposed by quantum algorithms, dives into emerging quantum-safe solutions, and tackles the messy reality of moving to a \gls{pqc}-secure world.
